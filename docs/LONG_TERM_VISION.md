# EthosFi — Long-Term Vision Note

**Status: FUTURE VISION — not a current priority, not to influence any near-term decision.**
**Added: July 2026. Revisit only after Step 4 below is achieved.**

---

## Why this note exists

This captures a long-term direction for EthosFi that surfaced during a broker approach for a Danish EMI acquisition (declined — see context below). The acquisition itself was rejected as premature and financially unjustified at this stage. But the underlying long-term question it raised — "should EthosFi always be just a scoring engine?" — is worth preserving, explicitly separated from any near-term action.

## The current, non-negotiable sequence

Nothing below changes this order. Each step must be genuinely achieved before the next is pursued:

1. Get a first successful backtest with a CDFI.
2. Convert that backtest into a paid pilot.
3. Generate first real revenue.
4. Demonstrate, with evidence, that EthoScore measurably improves credit decision quality.
5. **Only then** — explore partnerships with banks, EMIs, lending platforms, or other regulated entities.

As of this note, EthosFi is at Step 1 (outreach sent to 11 CDFIs/credit unions, two personal referral leads in progress, no backtest data received yet).

## The long-term direction (Step 5+, horizon: condition-based, not date-based)

**Note: timing is not fixed at "2+ years" — it depends entirely on how fast Step 4 is genuinely reached. If the first backtest, pilot, and proof-of-value happen quickly, this conversation can happen sooner. The gate is evidence, not a calendar.**

EthosFi does not need to remain solely a standalone scoring engine sold directly to CDFIs. Plausible longer-term shapes, once real traction exists:

- **Decision engine for CDFIs** — the current, proven use case, deepened.
- **AI compliance layer** — broader regulatory/explainability infrastructure beyond credit scoring alone.
- **Risk layer across multiple financial institutions** — a shared intelligence layer, not a single-tenant tool.
- **Embedded intelligence inside banks, EMIs, lending platforms, or fintechs** — EthoScore as a white-label/API layer integrated into a partner's existing, regulated infrastructure.

## The critical distinction: partnership, not acquisition

The declined Danish EMI deal was an **acquisition** (EthosFi buying regulated payment infrastructure, €1M, immediate). That is explicitly rejected — wrong stage, wrong capital allocation, wrong focus, and a repeat of a previously-abandoned embedded-finance/EMI pivot from 2025 that did not work out.

The long-term direction above is the opposite shape: **EthosFi supplies intelligence to a partner's existing infrastructure — EthosFi never owns or operates the regulated entity.** A bank, EMI, or lending platform would integrate EthoScore as a decision layer, keeping their own licensing, compliance, and operations intact.

## Revenue model reference (added for pitch/planning use — not a commitment or forecast)

Pricing philosophy: the free backtest stays free — it is the entry offer and should never be paid. Only the ongoing pilot/production usage is priced, because sustainable pricing is what lets EthosFi keep serving CDFIs at all (an indefinitely free tool is not a credible long-term partner in a regulated lender's eyes, and has no path to survival).

Rough reference figures (UK CDFI/credit union context, July 2026 pipeline of 11 organizations):

| Scenario | Basis | Illustrative monthly revenue | Illustrative annual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot phase, 3 early converts | ~£300-500/client/month | ~£1,300/month | ~£15,600/year |
| Year 2, same 3 clients at production volume | ~£800-2,500/client/month | ~£4,000-5,700/month | ~£48,000-68,000/year |
| 7 of 11 current pipeline convert | ~£900/client/month avg | ~£6,300/month | ~£75,600/year |
| 15 clients (pipeline + expansion), 24 months | ~£1,200/client/month avg | ~£18,000/month | ~£216,000/year |

The ~£200k ARR range is roughly the threshold that makes a UK B2B startup credible for a Seed round (£1-3M). This is illustrative, not a forecast — the real lever is pipeline conversion rate, not client count in the abstract.

## What would need to be true before revisiting this

- At least one completed, well-documented backtest with measurable results.
- At least one paying pilot client.
- Evidence (not projection) that EthoScore changes real credit decisions for the better.
- Inbound interest from a regulated partner — not EthosFi actively shopping for acquisition targets or partnership deals.
